3 · Read & play

The week and the playbook

Everything in this course was practice for an ordinary week. Now run one:

Sunday: split the week (lesson 6) — every assignment and obligation broken into sitting-sized tasks with dates, each one sorted into its bucket (lesson 1): delegate, collaborate, never, or no AI at all.

All week: work the plan. Tutor sessions before anything test-like (lesson 3). Critique mode — never ghostwriting — on anything you write (lesson 4). The verify pass on anything factual before you repeat it (lesson 5). Your templates for the repeats (lesson 7). Steer, show examples, restart when a chat sours (lesson 2).

The log is the assignment. For each real AI interaction: what you asked, what came back, and what you did with it — kept it, fixed it, or threw it out. The throwaways and failures are required. A log with no failures in it is a log of someone who didn't use the tool much, or didn't look hard at what it returned.

Friday: write the playbook — one page, in your own voice: your buckets with your examples, your tutor prompt, your templates, your never-list, and the one rule you'd give a friend who's starting out. That page is the actual product of this course, and it should change again by spring.
