3 · Read & play

Three buckets

People get AI wrong in both directions. Some treat it like a genius that's always right. Some treat it like a toy that's always wrong. Both groups lose. The model is a partner: unbelievably fast at some jobs, quietly unreliable at others, and it never tells you which is which. That's your job.

What it's reliably good at: producing drafts and options fast, explaining things at whatever level you ask, quizzing you, spotting patterns in text you paste, and never getting tired or annoyed at question number forty.

Where it fails: facts it can't check (it will state a wrong date as calmly as a right one), anything about your life it can't see, judgment calls that are yours to make, and taking responsibility — it doesn't sit in your class or carry your grade.

So every task gets sorted into one of three buckets before you type anything:

Delegate — hand it over. Safe when the stakes are low and you can check the result yourself in seconds. Brainstorms, practice quizzes, explanations.

Collaborate — split the work. It drafts and you decide, or you draft and it critiques. Anything that goes out under your name lives here.

Never — keep it. Graded work presented as yours, decisions about people, anything you couldn't verify or defend. If you can't check it, don't delegate it.

4 · Try it

Sort your actual week

Write down ten real tasks from your week — homework, messages, planning, studying, anything. Sort each one into delegate / collaborate / never, using the two tests from the reading: can I check the result? and does this carry my name or affect a person?

Then actually delegate one of your delegate tasks and collaborate on one collaborate task. Notice how different the two interactions feel.

Go deeper — optional, for when the lesson wasn't enough

More on the three buckets, if you want it

Your chatbot probably has a study mode. The major AI chatbots now ship learning modes that quiz instead of answer. Find yours — look for "study" or "learning" in the settings — and compare it with the buckets from this lesson.
Stretch. Sort your actual current week — every task you owe school — into delegate, collaborate, never. Keep the list; week 4's capstone starts from it.

Your triage list
