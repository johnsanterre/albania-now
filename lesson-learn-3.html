Leveraging AI in Your Own Education · Week 3 of 4 · about 2–4 hours

Write with it — honestly

There are two ways to point an AI at your writing. One makes you a better writer. The other makes you replaceable, and usually gets caught anyway.

3 · Read & play

Keep the pen

Path one: the ghostwriter. "Rewrite this paragraph and make it good." Back comes something smooth, correct — and hollow. It doesn't sound like you, because it isn't you. It sounds like the average of everything, because that's literally what the model is. Teachers read thirty essays a week; the AI-flavored ones announce themselves. And even when they don't: the work isn't yours, the grade isn't earned, and you didn't get better at the one skill the assignment existed to build.

Path two: the coach. The pen stays in your hand and the AI attacks your draft:

"Point at my three weakest sentences and say why each is weak."
"Argue against my thesis as hard as you can."
"Where does my logic skip a step?"
"Which paragraph should go first, and why?"

Then you rewrite. Every word of the revision is yours; the AI just showed you where to dig. That's the same thing a great editor does, and no one calls a human editor cheating.

Voice is the tell. Your writing has fingerprints — the way you overuse dashes, your slightly-too-casual transitions, the joke you couldn't resist. A ghostwritten paragraph has no fingerprints. Guard yours; it's the only thing in the essay that can't be generated.

The line, in one test: could you defend every sentence as yours, out loud, to the teacher who assigned it? If yes, you used a coach. If no, you used a ghostwriter.

Disclosure: know your school's actual policy — they differ. And when a teacher allows AI help, one honest sentence covers you: "I drafted this myself; I used AI to critique the draft and revised it based on that feedback." If writing that sentence would feel like a confession, that's your answer about what you did.

Interactive · Figure 1

One paragraph, two paths

A real draft paragraph. Send it down each path.

My draft: "Lake Michigan is basicly a huge part of why Chicago even exists. The city grew because boats could bring stuff here. Even today the lake does a lot for the city, like drinking water and beaches and cooling. People forget how much they depend on it."

Run both. The difference you can feel at the end is the difference between losing your voice and finding it.

4 · Try it

Both paths, your paragraph

Take a paragraph you actually wrote recently. Run the ghostwriter path in one chat and the coach path in another. Then write your own revision using only the critique.

Read all three out loud: original, ghostwritten, your revision. Which one still sounds like a person — and which person?

Go deeper — optional, for when the lesson wasn't enough

More on the honesty line, if you want it

Find your school's actual policy. Ask a teacher what the school's AI rule is, in writing. If there isn't one, that is a conversation worth starting — bring this lesson's disclosure sentence as a proposal.
Stretch. Take one paragraph you already wrote and run it down both paths — ghostwriter ("rewrite this") and coach ("critique this, do not rewrite"). Put the results side by side. Decide which version you could defend out loud, sentence by sentence.

None of this is required for the sprint. Mark this segment complete either way.
