Subject: Request for non-financial support for Albania Now, a free education program for Albanian high-school students

What Albania Now is

Albania Now connects Albanian high-school students directly with working scientists and engineers. A student completes a four-week "sprint" of free online material on one topic — data science, planetary exploration, large language models, and others — and earns a seat in a live video lecture with the sprint's scientist, where students ask questions and hear answers in real time. Lectures are recorded and published. Completion also earns a certificate with a public verification link and a physical sticker, mailed to the student's school.

The program is optional, sits outside the normal curriculum, and is delivered in English. Seven sprints exist today, with material live at santerre.ai/albania-now. Confirmed speakers include Dr. Conor Nixon, Associate Laboratory Chief of the Planetary Systems Laboratory at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; Dr. Nedelina Teneva, machine-learning research scientist, PhD University of Chicago; and Dr. John Santerre, machine-learning faculty at UC Berkeley and SMU.

Students engage through more than one door: structured sprints for students who want a path; short programming games and weekly challenges for students who start by playing; the lectures and school ceremonies as public occasions; and a moderated online community. Every door leads to the same place — a student's completed work earning a scientist's attention.

What it costs Albania

Nothing. The program is free for students and schools, permanently. Free Focus funds the platform; operating costs are modest1 and are not part of this request. Teachers are not asked to grade, administer, or manage accounts — completion is verified by the platform itself.

What we ask — non-financial support

  1. A letter of endorsement from the Ministry, so that school directors may share the program with students with confidence in its legitimacy.
  2. Distribution through official channels — one circular to high-school directors describing the program, with the Ministry's knowledge attached.
  3. Computer-lab access — the Ministry's encouragement that schools lend lab time to students whose only device is a phone, for the sprints that need a keyboard.
  4. A named liaison in the Ministry for coordination, and guidance on parental-consent requirements for minors under Albanian data-protection law, which the platform will implement as specified.
  5. Recognition of the certificate as a documented extracurricular achievement; a Ministry co-signature on certificates is welcome but not required.
  6. Amplification at launch — mention through Ministry channels and public media, timed to the first cohort's closing lecture.

What the Ministry gains

  • A national program of STEM and English exposure for high-school students, at zero cost to the state budget.
  • Direct contact between Albanian students and scientists at institutions including NASA Goddard and UC Berkeley — contact earned by completed work, and recorded lectures that remain public afterward.
  • Quarterly reporting: participation by school and region, completion counts, and lecture attendance, shared with the Ministry in full.
  • Alignment with Albania's digital agenda, alongside existing initiatives such as TUMO Tirana that have shown the appetite among Albanian youth for technology education.

Safeguards

The platform collects a student's name, school, year, and email — nothing else. Stickers ship to schools, never to home addresses. Parental consent is collected for minors before any account is created. Community spaces are moderated by named adults; recordings default to chat questions read aloud by a moderator, so no student appears on camera without consent. Data is deleted on request. There are no fees, no advertisements, and no sale of data — the platform has nothing to sell.
