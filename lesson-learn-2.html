3 · Read & play

Make it quiz you

Here's the trap every student falls into: rereading and highlighting feel productive because the material looks familiar. Familiar is not the same as known. The thing that actually builds memory is retrieval — being forced to pull an answer out of your own head, struggling for two seconds, and finding out immediately whether you had it. The struggle is not a sign it's failing. The struggle is the mechanism.

This is where the AI becomes genuinely powerful: it's an infinitely patient quizmaster. But out of the box it behaves like a helpful friend who blurts answers. You have to configure it. The working tutor prompt has five settings:

One question at a time. Otherwise it dumps twenty and you skim.
Wait for my answer. No answer key until you've committed.
Tell me exactly what I got wrong. Not "close!" — what was missing.
Get harder as I improve. Easy questions stop teaching fast.
Use only my pasted notes. This one is non-negotiable, and here's why: the model can invent facts with total confidence. Studying invented facts is worse than not studying. Paste in your notes, your study guide, your textbook section — and tell it to quiz from that and nothing else.

Second technique: explain it back. Tell the AI "I'm going to explain photosynthesis to you like you're a freshman. Grade my explanation and list what I got wrong or skipped." Teaching a thing is the fastest way to find the holes in your version of it.

Third: hunt your gaps. "Here's my study guide. Ask me the three questions I'm most likely to miss." It's unreasonably good at finding them.

4 · Try it

A real session for a real test

Pick your nearest actual test or quiz. Paste your notes or study guide into a chat, set up the tutor with all five settings, and run at least ten questions.

Then run one explain-it-back: teach the AI your hardest topic and have it grade your explanation.

Rule for the whole exercise: commit to an answer before you see one. Out-loud counts. Skimming doesn't.

Go deeper — optional, for when the lesson wasn't enough

More on retrieval, if you want it

The science behind the struggle. retrievalpractice.org — a researcher-run site on why being quizzed beats rereading, with guides for students. The five tutor settings in this lesson come straight from these findings.
Stretch. Before your next real test, run one full tutor session with the five settings — and note which question you missed that rereading would never have caught.
