3 · Read & play

From counts to text

Your table can predict one next letter. To make it write, you need two small moves.

Move one: turn counts into probabilities. If, after t, the table saw h 40 times, o 10 times, and space 50 times, divide by the total: h gets 40%, o gets 10%, space gets 50%. Every row of the table becomes a set of betting odds that sums to one hundred percent.

Move two: roll the dice. Start with a letter. Look up its row. Pick the next letter randomly, weighted by the odds — h wins 40% of such rolls. Now you have a new letter; look up ITS row, roll again. Repeat a hundred times and the machine has written a line of text that has never existed anywhere.

The output is gibberish — but look closely at the gibberish. Word lengths look right. Vowels and consonants alternate mostly-plausibly. A q, when one appears, is followed by u. It's wrong the way English is wrong, not the way static is wrong — because everything it knows, it learned from real text. This loop — look up, roll, append, repeat — is exactly the loop the biggest models on earth run. What changes later is only how smart the "look up" step gets.
